Bloby Part 2 — Bring your character to life (calm, smooth motion)

What this does: gives you the prompt from the video. Claude writes a Blender Python script that animates a character by code: gentle breathing, a slow look-around, a small wave, and a mouth that opens and closes with a voice recording.

You need: a character like Bloby in Blender (see Part 1) and Claude. The character should have these objects: CharacterRoot (parent), Head, Mouth, Arm and Arm.001. If your names differ, tell Claude your names.

The prompt (copy everything below)

You are an expert Blender Python (bpy) animator. I have a cartoon character in my scene. Objects:
"CharacterRoot" (empty, parent of everything), "Head", "Mouth", "Arm" (left), "Arm.001" (right).
Write ONE complete script for Blender 4.x/5.x that animates it with keyframes (24 fps, 10 seconds)
with CALM, SMOOTH motion. Absolutely NO hopping, no dancing, no big bounces.

Animation rules:
1. Breathing: scale of CharacterRoot in Z changes by only about 1.5% in a slow sine (period ~3 s).
2. Idle: tiny float of CharacterRoot (about 1.5 cm) and a tiny rotation around Z (about 0.7 degrees).
3. Look around: from second 2 to second 5 the Head turns slowly left and right (max about 12 degrees),
   starting and ending smoothly (ease in/out, smoothstep).
4. Wave: from second 6 to second 8 the right arm "Arm.001" rises smoothly, waves gently about
   15 degrees back and forth, and comes down smoothly.
5. Talking mouth: add a function that reads a WAV file path from a variable at the top of the script,
   measures its loudness per frame (use the wave and audioop/array modules or numpy if available),
   smooths it (soft attack and release) and uses it to scale the Mouth in Z between 0.5x (closed)
   and 3.5x (wide open) so the lips match the voice. If numpy is not available, use the standard library.
6. Camera: a very slow push-in (about 10% closer) over the whole clip, ease in/out.
7. Set every F-curve interpolation to BEZIER with auto-clamped handles so nothing looks robotic.

Also: put every number (speed, strength, timing) in a small settings block at the top so I can tune it,
comment the code, and tell me in 3 short lines how to run it and how to render it as an mp4.

The trick from the video: ask → look → fix

My first version was way too bouncy. I looked at it, and then I told Claude exactly what was wrong:

"The motion is too bouncy. Make everything slower and calmer, remove the hops, and ease every movement in and out."

Good fix prompts are specific: say what you see and how much to change (for example "20% slower", "half the rotation"). Change one thing at a time.
